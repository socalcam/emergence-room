The rule: Every cell holds two chemicals — one is constantly fed in, the other eats it to make more of itself — and both endlessly leak into neighboring cells at different speeds.

Why nobody designed these patterns

Every specimen here runs on the same premise: local rules, global order. A slime-mold agent senses only three points ahead of itself. A cell in the spiral colony sees only its eight neighbors. A particle feels only the particles within a small radius. None of them holds a picture of the whole — yet networks route, spirals rotate, and galaxies of density condense.

This is the same trick biology uses. Leopard spots and zebrafish stripes are reaction–diffusion. Real Physarum polycephalum rebuilt the Tokyo rail map in a petri dish. Snowflakes and mineral dendrites are diffusion-limited aggregation. The mathematics in this room is the mathematics outside the window.

Each simulation is deterministic: the seed above fixes every random choice, so this exact collection — these particular corals, crystals, and storms — exists only for the machine that grew it. Regrow to meet the neighbors.
